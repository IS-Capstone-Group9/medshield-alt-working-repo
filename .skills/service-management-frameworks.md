# Service Management Frameworks

## Description
Provides the service-management framing used when MedShield needs operational structure around incidents, changes, support, and service levels. Use this file when a change affects how the team will run, support, or measure the service. The purpose is to keep service thinking practical: what will break, who responds, how it is restored, and how the team learns from the event. In the capstone, these frameworks help explain how the solution would be supported if it were operating as a real service instead of a class project.

## Workflow
1. Define the operational concern: incident, problem, change, request, knowledge, or service level.
2. Choose the ITSM practice that fits the situation.
3. Identify the metric or service expectation that will be affected.
4. Write the recommendation in a way the team can act on.
5. Capture any runbook, ownership, or follow-up work needed to support the service.
6. Translate the operational advice into language that fits the paper or project documentation.

## Rules
- Use ITIL 4 practices when they help structure the operational response.
- Keep incident, problem, and change concerns distinct.
- Tie service-level discussion to measurable outcomes such as SLA, MTTR, availability, or response time.
- Do not recommend service targets that the current system or team cannot realistically support.
- Make ownership and escalation explicit.
- Keep the guidance useful to operators, not just managers.
- Make sure operational recommendations still make sense in a capstone-sized team.
