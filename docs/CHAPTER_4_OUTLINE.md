# Chapter 4: Results and Discussion

This chapter presents the empirical findings, quantitative outputs, validation metrics, and analytical insights generated by the MedShield Decision Support System (DSS) analytics pipeline. The results are structured to follow the progressive analytical paths defined in the MedShield North Star Diagram: beginning with raw data ingestion and descriptive baseline statistics, moving to predictive modeling, and concluding with prescriptive optimization. 

Each subsection details the underlying mathematical methodologies, provides templates for required data visualizations (Figures) and data summaries (Tables), and outlines the academic discussion required to contextualize the findings against the primary research objective: optimizing inventory levels and reducing stock losses.

## 4.2. Descriptive Analytics Findings [North Star 2A – 7A]

### 4.2.1. Business Taxonomy & Service Contract Allocations [Node 2A]
* **Description:** Present the results of mapping raw transactional data into structured categories (provinces, channels, and product groups).
* **Mathematical & Operational Rules Applied:**
    * Automated keyword scanning logic (identifying office supplies vs. medical items).
    * **Backward-Allocation Algorithm:** Bulk service contracts (identified by # in the product name) are decomposed into estimated child products based on the historical product-mix weight of that channel and territory over the preceding three years. Weight of SKU = (Sum of SKU Quantity in channel and territory) / (Total Quantity in channel and territory).
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Table 4.3: Bulk Contract Decomposition Examples:** Show a sample parent bulk contract row and its five resulting child rows flagged as estimated_backward_allocation.
* **Academic Discussion:** Evaluate how rule-based taxonomy mapping separates operational overhead from clinical supply data, and analyze the validity of using historical product-mix weights to estimate bulk contract components.

### 4.2.2. Historical Seasonality Analysis [Node 3A]
* **Description:** Analyze seasonal fluctuations across product classes.
* **Mathematical & Operational Rules Applied:**
    * **Seasonality Index (SI)** calculation for calendar month: SI = Monthly Average Volume / Overall Monthly Baseline Sales Volume.
    * **Seasonal Strength (SS)** metric: SS = (Max SI - Min SI) / Max SI.
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Figure 4.2: Monthly Seasonality Index (SI) for Key Product Categories:** Line chart showing seasonal demand indexes (e.g., Dengue-treatment medicines peaking from June to October).
* **Academic Discussion:** Interpret the clinical and operational drivers behind the seasonal coefficients. Highlight how these patterns correspond to environmental factors (e.g., monsoon seasons driving vector-borne illness treatments).

### 4.2.3. Revenue Contribution and Pareto Concentration [Node 4A]
* **Description:** Determine the distribution of revenue across the product portfolio.
* **Mathematical & Operational Rules Applied:**
    * Cumulative revenue ranking (80/20 analysis): Cumulative Share = Running Revenue Sum / Total Revenue.
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Figure 4.3: Lorenz Curve of Product Revenue Concentration:** Chart illustrating cumulative revenue share vs. cumulative product share.
    * **Table 4.4: Pareto Classification Summary (Class A, B, and C SKUs):** Summary statistics including SKU count, percentage of portfolio, and revenue share for each class.
* **Academic Discussion:** Examine the degree of revenue concentration. Discuss the risk profile of having a small subset of Class A products drive the vast majority of cash flow.

### 4.2.4. YoY Growth Trends & Geographic Performance [Nodes 5A & 6A]
* **Description:** Analyze growth rates and identify high-performing territories.
* **Mathematical & Operational Rules Applied:**
    * **Year-over-Year Growth Rate (YoY):** YoY = (Sales_current - Sales_prior_year) / Sales_prior_year * 100.
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Table 4.5: Territory Performance Leaderboard:** List provinces ranked by total revenue, net margin, and average YoY growth rate.
    * **Figure 4.4: Geographic Distribution of Revenue and Profit Margin:** GIS map or choropleth visual highlighting territorial performance.
* **Academic Discussion:** Synthesize the performance variations across regions, identifying regions with high revenue but low margin (indicating high shipping or operating costs).

### 4.2.5. Customer Concentration Analysis [Node 7A]
* **Description:** Evaluate the distribution of sales across different customer types (Government, Hospitals, and Pharmacies).
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Figure 4.5: Revenue and Margin Share by Channel Segment:** Pie charts displaying sales volume vs. profit margin contributions per channel.
* **Academic Discussion:** Discuss the strategic implications of customer concentration, such as the volatility of government tender cycles compared to steadier, higher-margin pharmacy sales.

## 4.3. Predictive Analytics & Forecast Evaluation [North Star 2B – 7B]

### 4.3.1. Monthly Product Demand Baseline Forecasts [Node 2B]
* **Description:** Present the baseline time-series forecasts generated for key SKUs.
* **Mathematical Model:**
    * **Prophet Additive Model Formulation:** y(t) = g(t) + s(t) + h(t) + e(t) where g(t) is trend, s(t) is seasonality, h(t) is holiday effects, and e(t) is error.
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Figure 4.6: 12-Month Out-of-Sample Demand Forecasts for Select Class A SKUs:** Plot showing 2026 actuals, 2027 forecasts, and the 95% uncertainty interval bounds.
* **Academic Discussion:** Evaluate the trend component for key products to determine if baseline demand is structurally expanding, plateauing, or decaying.

### 4.3.2. Forecast Accuracy Evaluation [Node 3B]
* **Description:** Compare the Prophet model's predictions to actual performance.
* **Mathematical Metrics:**
    * **Mean Absolute Percentage Error (MAPE):** MAPE = (100% / n) * Sum of |(Actual - Forecast) / Actual|.
    * **Root Mean Squared Error (RMSE):** RMSE = Square Root of (Average of squared errors).
    * **Mean Absolute Error (MAE):** MAE = Average of absolute errors.
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Table 4.6: Out-of-Sample Validation Performance Metrics (Prophet vs. Naive Seasonal Baseline):** Present comparative error metrics across Class A, B, and C SKUs.
* **Academic Discussion:** Analyze why the Prophet model outperforms the naive benchmark, particularly in seasonal classes, and discuss the limitations of forecasting low-volume Class C items.

### 4.3.3. Disease & Weather Regressors [Nodes 4B & 5B]
* **Description:** Measure the impact of incorporating meteorological and epidemiological signals into the time-series model.
* **Mathematical & Operational Rules Applied:**
    * Integration of the Disease Load Index (DLI) and Rainfall Severity Index (RSI) as dynamic regressors in the Prophet formulation.
    * **Weather-Seasonal Fallback Rule:** Fallback logic that downgrades the model to historical seasonal averages in the absence of real-time DOH or PAGASA data uploads.
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Table 4.7: Regressor Coefficients and Model Error Reduction:** Show the regression coefficients for DLI and RSI, and the percentage reduction in MAPE compared to the baseline Prophet model.
* **Academic Discussion:** Quantify the value of external signal integrations. Discuss how weather and epidemiological factors serve as leading indicators for pharmaceutical demand.

### 4.3.4. Cold-Start Prioritization & Future SKU Classification [Nodes 6B & 7B]
* **Description:** Present machine learning results for priority scoring and classification of new or low-history products.
* **Mathematical Model:**
    * **XGBoost Classifier:** Tree ensemble model trained to predict future ABC classes and output continuous probability-based Demand Urgency Scores.
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Figure 4.7: XGBoost Classification Confusion Matrix:** Grid showing actual vs. predicted ABC classes.
    * **Table 4.8: Top 10 Cold-Start SKUs by Demand Urgency Score:** List of new products flagged by XGBoost as requiring immediate inventory attention.
* **Academic Discussion:** Discuss how the machine learning classifier addresses the "cold-start" problem, enabling the system to classify products and assign urgency scores before historical sales records are established.

## 4.4. Prescriptive Optimization & Decision-Support Outputs [North Star 2C – 8C]

### 4.4.1. Inventory Control Parameters [Nodes 2C & 3C]
* **Description:** Present the cost-minimized replenishment parameters.
* **Mathematical Formulas:**
    * **Economic Order Quantity (EOQ):** EOQ = Square Root of (2 * Annual Demand * Ordering Cost / Holding Cost).
    * **Safety Stock (SS):** SS = Service Factor * Standard Deviation of Lead-Time Demand.
    * **Reorder Point (ROP):** ROP = (Average Daily Demand * Lead Time) + Safety Stock.
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Table 4.9: Calculated Replenishment Parameters for High-Value SKUs:** Details of EOQ, Safety Stock, and ROP values per product.
* **Academic Discussion:** Assess how the calculated ROP values protect against stockouts during lead-time delays, and discuss the financial trade-offs between holding costs and ordering costs.

### 4.4.2. Emergency Alert Overrides [Nodes 4C & 5C]
* **Description:** Validate the system's response to extreme disease outbreaks and weather events.
* **Mathematical & Operational Rules Applied:**
    * **Outbreak Trigger Condition:** Raise alert if weekly cases exceed mean cases + (2 * standard deviation of cases).
    * **Typhoon Override Rule:** Automatically double safety stock levels (New Safety Stock = 2 * Safety Stock) for all warehouses within the storm warning path.
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Figure 4.8: Safety Stock Simulation Under Storm Warning Scenarios:** Diagram showing the baseline inventory path vs. the adjusted path during a storm warning.
* **Academic Discussion:** Discuss the operational value of incorporating rule-based overrides into optimization models to handle extreme tail-risk events.

### 4.4.3. Constrained Stock Allocation Optimization [Nodes 2C & 6C]
* **Description:** Present the regional prioritization rankings and optimized stock allocation matrices under constraints.
* **Mathematical Models:**
    * **MCDA Scoring Model:** Weighted scoring of territories: Score = Sum of (Weight * Normalized Criterion Value).
    * **Linear Programming (LP) Optimization Model:** Minimize Transportation Costs, or Maximize Prioritized Demand Filled, subject to Allocated Stock being less than or equal to Total Inventory Available, and Allocated Stock being less than or equal to Warehouse Capacity.
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Table 4.10: MCDA Territory Prioritization Rankings:** Criteria weights and final scores by province.
    * **Table 4.11: Optimized Allocation Matrix (LP Output):** Allocated quantities per product and region, highlighting active binding constraints.
* **Academic Discussion:** Interpret the LP results. Analyze the shadow prices of active binding constraints (such as budget limits and storage capacity) to identify the highest-return investments for future resource expansions.

### 4.4.4. Collaborative Filtering for Product-Region Expansion [Node 7C]
* **Description:** Present recommendations for expanding products into new territories.
* **Mathematical Model:**
    * **Cosine Similarity:** Similarity = (Vector A dot Vector B) / (Magnitude of Vector A * Magnitude of Vector B).
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Table 4.12: Top Recommended Product-Territory Pairings:** Recommended product expansions paired with their cosine similarity scores.
* **Academic Discussion:** Explain how similarity-based recommendations assist sales and business development teams by identifying latent demand patterns in comparable markets.

### 4.4.5. Stop-Purchasing Flags [Node 8C]
* **Description:** Present the identification of inactive or dead stock.
* **Mathematical & Operational Rules Applied:**
    * **Flagging Condition:** Product is tagged as STOP_PURCHASING if it belongs to Class C and has zero transaction velocity over the trailing 6 months.
* **Quantitative Outputs:**
    * **Table 4.13: Dead Stock SKUs Proposed for Purchase Halt:** List of flagged SKUs, current inventory value, and projected holding cost savings.
* **Academic Discussion:** Discuss the supply chain implications of implementing a stop-purchasing flag, focusing on warehouse capacity recovery and capital reallocation.

## 4.5. Research Limitations
* **Coverage Constraints:** Document the limitations caused by the gaps in official PAGASA weather datasets (ending in 2024) and DOH disease registries (ending in 2025).
* **Proxy Data:** Discuss the utilization of NASA POWER/Open-Meteo observations as weather proxies in place of live, high-frequency municipal weather warning feeds.
* **Scenario-Based Demonstration:** Address how missing operational parameters in the client's legacy database require the prescriptive recommendations to be interpreted as scenario-based decision-support simulations rather than finalized operational execution schedules.
