Respiratory illness: Philippine influenza studies found a June–November season aligned with the rainy season, but the
ones found use 2006–2013 data ([national surveillance, *BMC Infectious Diseases*](https://bmcinfectdis.biomedcentral.com/articles/10.1186/s12879-016-2087-9);
[subtropical–tropical Asia, *PLOS ONE*](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0167712)). Cite as
background only, or search further for 2021+ work.

Pre-2021 Philippine studies on cholera rainfall and humidity links ([scoping review 2019](https://doi.org/10.3390/ijerph16142624))
and the 2018 Manila rainfall–flood–leptospirosis study ([PLOS NTD](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pntd.0006331))
are foundational and widely cited; they can appear as background, outside the 5-year set.

Note for the paper: Philippine evidence is strongest for dengue and leptospirosis, moderate for typhoid, and mixed for
cholera (some studies find rainfall effects, one finds a hot-season peak). Typhoid and diarrhea are supported mainly by
regional reviews.

Burden context (non-seasonal demand):
- [IHME GBD 2021 Philippines profile](https://www.healthdata.org/sites/default/files/2025-06/IHME_ASEAN_template_Philippines_final.pdf): leading causes include ischemic heart disease, stroke, lower respiratory infection, diabetes, CKD and tuberculosis; top risks high blood pressure, air pollution, smoking, high blood sugar. Check rank order on the original.
- [Revisiting the burden of NCDs in the Philippines, CPBRD 2025](https://cpbrd.congress.gov.ph/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/DP2025-01-REVISITING-THE-BURDEN-OF-NONCOMMUNICABLE-DISEASES-IN-THE-PHILIPPINES_Final.pdf): NCDs are 8 of the 10 leading causes of death.
- [Random forest for NCD essential-medicine demand, Rwanda, *Pan African Medical Journal* 2022](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9379432/): disease-linked medicine demand forecasting in public facilities.
- [Pharmaceutical policy editorial 2023 (PMC)](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC9971951/): rising NCD prevalence and ageing drive 3–6% annual medicine demand growth.

Data note: WHO FluNet has Philippine influenza, RSV and SARS-CoV-2 detections by week; news reports of DOH ILI totals
conflict (179,227 in 2024 vs 216,786 in 2023 per Philstar; 193,148 to 2 Dec 2023 per Inquirer), so use the primary
dataset, not news.

Implication for the study: demand for MedShield's products likely has three drivers, which the RRL should separate:
(1) seasonal, climate-sensitive infections (dengue, leptospirosis, typhoid, respiratory); (2) steady chronic disease
(diabetes, CKD, cardiovascular), which grows slowly and is not seasonal; (3) programme and tender cycles (vaccines,
maternal care, government procurement). Our results so far match (3) most strongly.

## B. Medicine sales as a disease signal (supports the disease → demand hypothesis)

| Source | Year | Finding (to verify) | Use in paper |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Anticipating influenza-like illness outbreaks via OTC drug sales and primary care data, *npj Digital Public Health*](https://www.nature.com/articles/s44482-026-00014-w) | 2026 | 510 Brazilian regions, Nov 2022–Jun 2025; OTC sales gave 1–3 weeks' warning of respiratory surges | Strongest recent evidence that disease moves medicine sales |
| [Using OTC retail medication sales to detect and track influenza-like illnesses (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13686242) | 2025 | US retail data from 28,000+ outlets estimates daily ILI | Retail sales track disease |
| [Sales and product review data for disease surveillance](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/376514106_Sales_and_product_review_data_for_disease_surveillance) | ~2023 | Review of sales data for surveillance | Background |

Important contrast for Chapter 5: all of these use **retail / OTC point-of-sale** data, where patients buy when sick.
MedShield sells mainly to government offices and hospitals through large orders. No 2021–2026 study was found on
dengue and pharmacy sales, or on disease signals in institutional distribution, which is a gap this study addresses.
