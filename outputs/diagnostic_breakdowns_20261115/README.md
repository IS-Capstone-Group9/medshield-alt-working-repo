# Diagnostic breakdowns: margin decline and sales change, 15 November 2026

Offline, read-only accounting breakdowns of recorded figures (`run.py`). No models or estimates: each split adds up exactly
to the total change. Original itemized sales, contracts excluded, 2017 to September 2026; families from candidate mapping
v1.1. Margin uses only rows where cost and sales both pass quality checks, before operating expenses.

## A. Why gross margin fell from 70.7% (2019) to 49.4% (2025)

| Breakdown by | Mix effect (selling different things) | Rate effect (lower margin on the same things) |
|---|---|---|
| Product category | −2.1 pts | −19.2 pts |
| Channel label | −3.4 pts | −17.9 pts |
| **Product family** | **−17.1 pts** | **−4.2 pts** |

The finest level, product family, gives the most accurate picture:

- **Mostly a change in what MedShield sells.** High-margin families shrank or stopped selling, and newer families carry
  lower margins. Looking only at "medicine" makes this look like a rate drop, because the change happens inside the
  category.
- **The biggest single drags:**

| Family | Share of margin-eligible sales, 2019 → 2025 | Margin, 2019 → 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| SPEEDA | 9.2% → 2.6% | 86% → 33% |
| EVAPROST | 6.4% → 1.0% | 61% → 27% |
| ZINACARE | 3.9% → 2.7% | 92% → 48% |
| EUROXONE | 2.9% → 0.0% | 76% → 62% |
| LACTAMOX | 3.2% → 0.1% | 64% → 63% |

- **Some same-product margins also fell sharply** (SPEEDA 86% → 33%, ZINACARE 92% → 48%), which points to price or cost
  changes on those products.
- **Government sales:** margin on the GOVERNMENT label fell from 79% to 46% while its share rose, consistent with lower-margin
  tender products.
- **When it happened:** the largest drops were 2022→2023 (−11.3 pts, mostly same-product margins) and 2024→2025
  (−7.3 pts, mostly product mix).

**Caveat:** in 2019 only 65.9% of sales had usable cost data, against 90.3% in 2025. If the 2019 rows without cost had
lower margins, the 2019 figure is overstated and the true decline is smaller. Questions for the client: did prices on
SPEEDA and ZINACARE fall or did their costs rise, and why did SPEEDA, EVAPROST, EUROXONE and LACTAMOX shrink?

## B. What drove each year's sales change

Only months recorded in both years are compared.

| Years (matched months) | Change | Large orders | Continuing products | New products | Lost products |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2019→2020 (9) | −₱20.6M | −₱1.3M | −₱14.9M | +₱5.2M | −₱9.6M |
| 2020→2021 (12) | +₱24.8M | +₱11.2M | +₱5.0M | +₱14.3M | −₱6.1M |
| 2021→2022 (12) | −₱14.6M | −₱13.1M | −₱7.5M | +₱12.6M | −₱6.7M |
| 2022→2023 (12) | −₱1.0M | +₱4.2M | −₱5.8M | +₱12.6M | −₱12.0M |
| 2023→2024 (12) | −₱33.8M | −₱16.3M | −₱5.2M | +₱5.5M | −₱17.9M |
| 2024→2025 (12) | **+₱60.1M** | **+₱36.7M** | +₱7.3M | +₱21.1M | −₱5.1M |
| 2025→2026 (Jan–Sep) | **+₱75.7M** | **+₱51.0M** | +₱0.7M | +₱36.2M | −₱12.2M |

Routine sales are split by product family; large orders are documents of ₱500,000 or more. 2017→2018 and 2018→2019 are
omitted here because only 5 and 7 months are comparable.

- **The recent growth comes from large orders and new products.** In January–September 2026, large orders explain
  ₱51.0M of the ₱75.7M increase and new product families ₱36.2M, while continuing routine products grew by only ₱0.7M.
- **The 2024 dip** came from lost product families (−₱17.9M) and fewer large orders (−₱16.3M).
- **Every year, new products add ₱5–36M and lost products take away ₱5–18M,** consistent with the portfolio turnover seen
  in the descriptive views.

## What this adds

Margin: the decline is mainly a shift away from high-margin products, plus price or cost changes on a few of them. Sales:
growth depends on winning large orders and adding new products, not on selling more of the same products. Both point to
the same structural change, from steady sales of established products to tender-driven institutional business.
